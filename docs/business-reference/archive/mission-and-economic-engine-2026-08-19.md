## Core thesis preserved from the source

The document framed Kleenest as more than a restroom finder: a **location-intelligence network** built around the universal problem of finding and evaluating usable public restrooms.

The intended system combined:
- restroom discovery,
- cleanliness intelligence,
- amenity data,
- accessibility information,
- verification,
- community contribution,
- business-side analytics.

The source described a low-friction consumer product feeding a larger data and B2B network.

## Economic engine

The source explicitly separated the consumer funnel from the B2B funnel:

### Consumer network
Registered consumers  
→ monthly active users  
→ daily active users  
→ ad impressions  
→ advertising revenue

Separately:

Registered consumers  
→ Premium conversion  
→ one-time consumer revenue

Then:

Consumer density  
→ participating/business locations  
→ Standard / Growth / Fleet  
→ recurring B2B revenue

The document's point was that **consumer density and engagement create the commercial network**, rather than consumer ARPU being the first optimization target.

## KPI hierarchy

The source proposed this hierarchy:

### Tier 1 — Network
- MAU
- DAU
- geographic coverage
- restroom coverage

### Tier 2 — Data
- contributions per active user
- verified locations
- freshness
- rating / observation density

### Tier 3 — Consumer monetization
- Premium conversion
- Family conversion
- advertising ARPU

### Tier 4 — B2B
- business conversion
- average revenue per account
- churn
- Fleet penetration

### Tier 5 — Network economics
- B2B revenue per 1,000 active consumers

This hierarchy remains useful as historical support for the later consumer-first strategy.

## Contextual / local advertising thesis

The document proposed contextual advertising as a distinct revenue category rather than relying only on generic ad-network inventory.

Example logic:

Restroom search  
→ current local intent  
→ nearby relevant need such as food, coffee, fuel, lodging, or retail  
→ sponsored local business

The source argued that advertising should derive from real consumer utility and contextual intent.

## Data asset envisioned

The source anticipated a location-intelligence asset containing signals such as:
- location / business association,
- amenities,
- accessibility,
- cleanliness,
- traffic / usage,
- operating hours,
- consumer sentiment,
- geographic demand,
- route relevance,
- business category,
- traveler / consumer intent,
- possible occupancy signals.

## Historical external audit preserved in the PDF

The same PDF included an August 2026 audit of the then-current app.

That audit found:
- ambitious technical scaffolding,
- contribution / QR / business infrastructure already present,
- strong alignment with the network/data thesis,
- but insufficient live restroom density at that moment,
- too much emphasis on gamification relative to the core discovery/data problem,
- significant cold-start risk.

Its main recommendation was to make data density and Maps usefulness the priority before expanding engagement layers.

That product-state assessment is historical and should **not** be treated as a current statement about the live Kleenest apps.

## Strategic value of this source

This document is useful because it captures an early version of principles that later became central to the company:

1. Consumer adoption before monetization optimization.
2. Data quality and freshness as first-class KPIs.
3. B2B value emerging from consumer/network density.
4. Contextual advertising as a downstream monetization layer.
5. The proprietary data/network layer as a long-term moat.
