## 1. Skandalaris strategy brief

Skandalaris recommended **Experts on Call mentorship plus weekly workshops/networking**. The immediate objective was validation, not fundraising: turn mentor and event access into stronger evidence, pilot partners, customer interviews, and sharper positioning.

### Mentor requests
- Consumer/customer validation and interview design
- Two-sided marketplace/network growth and geographic density
- Monetization sequencing: free Consumer → Premium → Business/Fleet/Enterprise → advertising
- Pilot design and success metrics
- Fundraising readiness: what proof should precede a raise

### Stage language
Kleenest was framed as **pre-revenue and validating adoption/pilot economics**. Product and data infrastructure were already substantial, but ingested place records were explicitly not customer traction.

Traction was defined as:
- active users,
- repeat use,
- verified observations,
- check-ins,
- pilot partners,
- retention,
- paid conversion.

### Mentor questions
1. Which current assumption is most dangerous, and how should it be tested in 30 days?
2. What evidence would make Kleenest investable?
3. Which St. Louis distribution wedge offers the fastest path to dense consumer usage?

Every Skandalaris interaction should produce at least one decision, experiment, qualified introduction, interview, or pilot next step.

## 2. Idea-validation workshop sheet

Five hypotheses were identified for pressure-testing:

1. Consumers will repeatedly use Kleenest when restroom/amenity information is more current and trustworthy than generic maps.
2. Consumer network density and verified observations can become the primary acquisition/defensibility engine.
3. Businesses will maintain practical amenity information when the workflow is low-friction and visibly useful.
4. Mobile-workforce employers will value restroom/location access as an employee benefit even before adopting operations features.
5. Trade associations/chambers can be efficient neutral channels for recruiting pilots without endorsing an unproven product.

### Evidence emerging at the time
- Delmar Main Street was evaluating an exploratory corridor/business pilot.
- BSCAI requested a conversation about the technology.
- AGC Missouri identified mobile-workforce relevance and a membership/networking path.
- Four Seasons remained interested but wanted evidence of other credible participants.
- Skandalaris recommended validation-focused mentorship/workshops.

### Workshop questions
- How do we distinguish strong demand from polite interest?
- What sample size/evidence is enough before changing product or pricing?
- Which hypothesis should be falsified first?
- How should interviews be structured to avoid leading respondents?

## 3. Marketing workshop sheet

### Primary problem
Public company credibility had lagged product development. At least one sophisticated outreach recipient had difficulty finding authoritative public information about Kleenest.

### Consumer-first objective
Build a recognizable promise around practical, verified, current restroom/location information; then use consumer density to pull businesses and employers into the network.

### Questions
- What is the clearest one-sentence consumer promise?
- Which low-budget St. Louis acquisition channels create geographic density rather than scattered downloads?
- How should Kleenest distinguish itself in search from similarly named cleaning products?
- What should the public landing page prove in the first 20 seconds?

## 4. STL in Motion networking sheet

The event was treated as a relationship-building opportunity with Greater St. Louis, Inc.

### 30-second positioning
Kleenest is a St. Louis-built consumer-first location platform focused on verified practical amenity information, beginning with restrooms and real-world needs. The near-term priority is local adoption and pilots—including mobile-workforce and business use cases—before harder fundraising.

### Best asks
- Which St. Louis sectors have the highest concentration of mobile workers and are open to pilots?
- Which business districts or visitor organizations are testing customer/visitor-experience tools?
- Who should Kleenest meet if the immediate goal is 2–3 measurable local pilots rather than fundraising?

### Avoid
Do not lead with every feature, ask vaguely for “connections,” or call ingested records customer traction.

## 5. Value-proposition workshop sheet

| Segment | Value proposition |
|---|---|
| Consumer | Find a suitable restroom or practical amenity with current, evidence-backed information when generic maps are not enough. |
| Consumer Premium | More powerful discovery, route planning, and convenience for frequent users. |
| Business | Control practical amenity information, engage verified visitors, and learn from real-world location activity. |
| Fleet / mobile workforce | Reliable restroom/location access plus affordable routing, task, arrival, and service-verification workflows. |
| Hospitality / events | Low-burden guest/attendee utility without forcing heavy QR or systems integration. |
| Enterprise | Scale location intelligence, workforce workflows, analytics, and administration across larger organizations. |

For every segment, test: **painful job → current workaround → why Kleenest → measurable outcome → smallest pilot**.
