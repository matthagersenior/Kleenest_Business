# Kleenest Opportunity Readiness, Funding & Data-Room Reference

_Source artifacts: Kleenest_Opportunity_Readiness_Playbook.docx and Kleenest_Opportunity_Command_Center.xlsx, September 2026._

This material predates the current v9 36-month model in several places. Its value is the **readiness process, diligence checklist, pilot proof system, and funding discipline**, not its older numerical forecasts.

## Strategic objective

Use St. Louis as the proving ground and convert substantial product/location-data infrastructure into evidence of:
- partner demand,
- user utility,
- repeat usage,
- verified observations,
- willingness to pay.

The strongest founder story separates **what is built** from **what is proven**.

## Opportunity ladder

| Opportunity | Type | Primary value | Correct use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Four Seasons St. Louis | Flagship pilot | Hospitality proof / brand-sensitive discovery | Earn a low-friction measurable pilot; do not imply partnership before approval |
| Cortex SQ1 | Founder-development program | Discipline, mentorship, business fundamentals | Use as validation forcing function, not investment |
| TechSTL AngelLAB | Investor-readiness program | Pitch refinement + angel access | Use after early evidence exists |
| Arch Grants | Non-dilutive funding | Capital + STL ecosystem | Build application/diligence readiness around measurable proof |
| St. Louis Arch Angels | Angel process | Seed capital + operators | Approach after pilot evidence supports screening/diligence |
| Cultivation Capital | VC / geospatial | Larger capital + geospatial fit | Relationship/milestone feedback first; capital after repeatable growth evidence |

## Proof system

| Proof layer | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Do users/businesses care? | Interview notes, repeated questions, workarounds, urgency |
| Product | Can Kleenest solve it reliably? | Deterministic demo, successful searches, correction workflow |
| Trust/data | Why is the answer better? | Source attribution, freshness, confidence, contradictions resolved |
| Adoption | Will people use it? | Active testers, searches, verified observations, repeat users, check-ins |
| Partner demand | Will organizations participate? | Pilot confirmations, LOIs, champions, continuation decisions |
| Business model | Will someone pay? | Price interviews, conversion, renewal/expansion intent |
| Scale | Can it repeat beyond STL? | Repeatable onboarding, expansion cost, source coverage, channel partners, unit economics |

Investor framing: **“St. Louis is the proving ground, not the market.”**

## Original 90-day proof targets

The Command Center tracked these as early validation goals:
- 25 pilot locations,
- 3 institutional/channel partners,
- 250 active local testers,
- 10 substantive partner meetings,
- 5 partner LOIs/pilot confirmations,
- 250 verified amenity observations,
- 250 QR/verified check-ins,
- 75 repeat users.

These are historical targets, not present-state claims.

## Funding-scenario discipline

The Command Center included illustrative dilution math:
- $50k on a $2M post-money SAFE ≈ 2.5% implied ownership before future dilution.
- $500k into a $3M pre-money priced round → ~$3.5M post-money and ~14.3% new-investor ownership.

The sheet explicitly flagged terms to inspect: board rights, liquidation preference, pro-rata, MFN/discount/cap, veto/protective provisions, exclusivity, ROFR, IP rights, personal guarantees, and information rights.

This was an educational founder-readiness calculator, **not legal/tax advice or a recommended financing structure**.

## Meeting operating system

Before each meeting:
1. Define one outcome.
2. Research the person/organization.
3. Write one clear ask.
4. Choose three relevant proof points.
5. Prepare five questions.
6. Prepare one likely objection and concise response.

Close every meeting with a concrete next step rather than “keep in touch.”
