# Kleenest Market Evidence, Competitive Framing & Sizing Notes

This file consolidates market/evidence material found across the Kleenest business-plan generations and research notes. Treat it as a research reference, not a substitute for re-checking external sources before publication.

## Restroom-impact evidence captured in prior Kleenest planning

Earlier plans cited the following evidence categories:

1. **2019 peer-reviewed restaurant research**  
   Used to support the proposition that restroom cleanliness can affect customer satisfaction and that restroom appearance is an important component of perceived cleanliness.  
   **Limit:** does not establish a precise revenue lift from a Kleenest score.

2. **2026 Marketing Science research using Yelp + NYC inspection data**  
   Used to support the proposition that reviews can contain meaningful hygiene information consumers directly experience and that demand responds to hygiene signals.  
   **Limit:** hygiene is broader than restroom condition.

3. **2026 Bradley survey of 1,020 U.S. adults**  
   Planning materials recorded 68% saying clean restrooms make them more likely to return/spend more and 86% expecting restroom quality to reflect business quality.  
   **Limit:** industry-sponsored self-report evidence, not causal sales data.

4. **Tork Vision Connect / smart-restroom category evidence**  
   Used to validate that commercial systems already expose traffic, dispenser, and location metadata to facility-management systems.  
   **Limit:** validates the category, not Kleenest product-market fit.

5. **Yelp Open Dataset**  
   Used to establish feasibility of large-scale review mining for restroom/hygiene mentions, issue categories, sentiment, and business-category analysis.  
   **Limit:** dataset licensing/allowed-use terms must be checked before commercial use.

## Kleenest research objective

Move beyond broad correlation by building a longitudinal first-party dataset linking, where available and permitted:
- restroom condition,
- issue type,
- freshness and confidence,
- service response,
- verified resolution,
- repeat visits / usage,
- customer sentiment,
- voluntary business operational or transaction outcomes.

## Competitive framing

| Category | Strength | Kleenest distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Google Maps / Yelp / general reviews | Broad discovery and business information | Restroom-specific, time-aware, verification-oriented, operationally actionable |
| Bathroom-finder apps | Simple restroom discovery | Evidence, trust/freshness, business network, QR, operations, access, Enterprise layers |
| Reputation / CX tools | Reviews, surveys, response workflows | Restroom-specific physical-world signals and service loop |
| Field-service software | Scheduling, dispatch, work orders | Specialized low-cost restroom/location utility + employee benefit + FSM-like on-ramp |
| Smart-restroom systems | Sensors, occupancy, supplies | Hardware-agnostic data/action layer connecting sensor events to consumer/business outcomes |

Adjacent software pricing (Jobber, Housecall Pro, Birdeye, etc.) was used in planning as **budget context only**. Re-check current pricing before external use.

## Market sizing discipline

Do **not** use raw imported location-record counts as sales TAM.

A September 9, 2026 internal planning snapshot cited approximately:
- 191,225 active location records,
- 19,373 records within 25 miles of 63101,
- 120,377 within 250 miles,
- 145,596 active external-source records across six sources,
- 10,557 locations with confidence rows.

Those figures describe coverage infrastructure, not verified prospects or customers.

Initial serviceable market should be calculated bottom-up:
1. qualify locations by category,
2. determine restroom availability / public-access relevance,
3. filter for consumer traffic and operating fit,
4. identify buyer type,
5. apply observed conversion by segment and plan tier.

A later national TAM should use:
**qualified public-facing U.S. locations × expected tier mix + Fleet accounts + Enterprise contracts + transaction/sponsorship economics**.

## Starting-traction caution

An older September 9 internal snapshot described substantial infrastructure but very early live traction, including a small number of non-demo profiles/businesses, limited discovery events, a handful of active QR codes, one check-in, and no user bathroom-verification observations at that moment.

The strategic consequence remains valid: **do not confuse built coverage with adoption**. Consumer active use, repeat use, verified observations, pilot participation, paid commitments, and conversion are the traction metrics.
